Faded colours aren’t in the image. Edits are kept per crop, size and palette, so changing other settings keeps them. Use Background, then click the background, to make it transparent.

Pixel art from any image

pixelimage turns a photo, cover or illustration into pixel art limited to a palette, as a starting point for your own sprite rather than a filter. Pick a size from 16 to 256 pixels and a palette from classic hardware (Game Boy, NES, Commodore 64, ZX Spectrum, PICO-8), from pixel artists (Endesga 32, DawnBringer, Sweetie 16) or any palette on Lospec. Or take the palette from the image itself, optionally at the colour depth of the SNES, Amiga or Mega Drive.

Then clean it up: remove the background with a click, cap the number of colours, add a 1px outline and touch up pixels with a pencil, fill and picker that only paint in the palette, with mirroring for symmetrical sprites and a grid. Hold C to compare with the photo. Export a PNG at 1× for Aseprite or scaled up for sharing, and the palette as a GIMP .gpl.

Tips

  • Crop tight around the subject: at 32 pixels, every pixel counts.
  • Dithering suits palettes with few colours, like the Game Boy's. With 32 colours or more it mostly adds speckle.
  • Photos and illustrations work best. Screenshots of text and dark gradients don't.
  • Everything runs in your browser: images are never uploaded.
